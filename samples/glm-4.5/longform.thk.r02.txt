四诊 refers to the four diagnostic methods in TCM:
1. 望诊 (Wàng zhěn) - Inspection/Observation: observing the patient's spirit (神), complexion (色), body shape (形态), tongue (舌象), etc.
2. 闻诊 (Wén zhěn) - Listening and Smelling: listening to the patient's voice, breathing, coughing, and smelling odors
3. 问诊 (Wèn zhěn) - Inquiry: asking about symptoms, medical history, lifestyle, etc. (十问 - ten questions)
4. 切诊 (Qiè zhěn) - Pulse-taking and Palpation: feeling the pulse (脉诊) and palpating the body (按诊)

合参 means combining/synthesizing all four methods together for comprehensive analysis.

The key principle is that these four methods should not be used in isolation but combined - "四诊合参" emphasizes that a physician must integrate information from all four diagnostic methods to make an accurate diagnosis (辨证 - syndrome differentiation). Each method has its limitations, so combining them provides a more complete picture of the patient's condition.

Historical origin: The concept traces back to classic texts like 《黄帝内经》 and was developed in 《难经》, and 司外揣内 (judging the interior from the exterior).

The rationale: 望闻问切 each has its scope and limitations. For example:
- 望诊 observes external manifestations
- 闻诊 captures sounds and smells
- 问诊 gathers subjective symptoms and history
- 切诊 detects pulse and physical changes

Only by combining all four can a physician fully understand the disease's nature, location, and the body's condition, then perform 辨证论治.

There's a saying: "望而知之谓之神，闻而知之谓之圣，问而知之谓之工，切而知之谓之巧" from 《难经》.

Also important: avoiding the mistake of relying on only one method, e.g., "舍脉从症" or "舍症从脉" in certain situations where symptoms and pulse conflict - the physician needs to judge which is more reliable.
