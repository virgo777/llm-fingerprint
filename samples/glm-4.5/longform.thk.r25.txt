四诊 refers to the four diagnostic methods in TCM:
1. 望诊 (Wàng zhěn) - Inspection/Observation - observing the patient's spirit (神), complexion (色), body shape (形态), tongue (舌象), etc.
2. 闻诊 (Wén zhěn) - Listening and Smelling - listening to voice, breathing, and smelling odors
3. 问诊 (Wèn zhěn) - Inquiry - asking about symptoms, medical history, lifestyle, etc.
4. 切诊 (Qiè zhěn) - Pulse-taking and Palpation - feeling the pulse (脉诊) and palpating the body (按诊)

合参 means combining/synthesizing all four methods together for comprehensive analysis.

Key points to cover:
- Definition: 四诊合参 is the fundamental diagnostic principle of TCM, requiring doctors to use all four diagnostic methods together and synthesize the information
- Why it's needed: Each method has limitations; symptoms can be misleading individually; combining them avoids one-sided judgments
- The concept of "四诊并重" - all four should be given equal weight, not just relying on pulse-taking or observation alone
- Historical context: Originated from classical texts like 《黄帝内经》, 《难经》, and systematically described in 《伤寒论》by 张仲景. There's a saying "望而知之谓之神，闻而知之谓之圣，问而知之谓之工，切而知之谓之巧" from 《难经》

- Warning against relying on single method: 《医宗金鉴》says something like "四诊合参，方为医林之正务" or similar. Also the principle that 切脉 alone cannot diagnose everything.
