四诊合参 refers to the four diagnostic methods in TCM:
1. 望诊 (Wàng zhěn) - Inspection/Observation: observing the patient's complexion, body shape, tongue, spirit (神), etc.
2. 闻诊 (Wén zhěn) - Listening and Smelling: listening to voice, breathing, and smelling odors
3. 问诊 (Wèn zhěn) - Inquiry: asking about symptoms, medical history, lifestyle, etc. (十问歌 - Ten Questions Song)
4. 切诊 (Qiè zhěn) - Pulse-taking and Palpation: feeling the pulse (脉诊) and palpating the body (按诊)

四诊合参 means combining and synthesizing all four diagnostic methods' information to make a comprehensive diagnosis (辨证), rather than relying on just one method. This is a core principle in TCM diagnostics.

The phrase comes from classic texts. 《难经》 mentions "望而知之谓之神，闻而知之谓之圣，问而知之谓之工，切脉而知之谓之巧" (knowing by inspection is called divine, by listening sagely, by asking skillful, by pulse-taking clever).

The importance of 四诊合参:
- Each method has limitations
- Only by combining all can one get a complete picture
- Avoids 偏诊误断
- Forms the basis for 辨证论治

Historical source: 《黄帝内经》 laid the foundation. The term 四诊 appears later, but the concept is from Neijing.
