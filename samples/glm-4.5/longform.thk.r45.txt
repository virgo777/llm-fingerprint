四诊合参 (Sì zhěn hé cān) refers to the comprehensive analysis of the four diagnostic methods in TCM:

1. 望诊 (Wàng zhěn) - Inspection/Observation: Observing the patient's spirit (神), complexion (色), body shape (形态), tongue (舌象), etc. This includes 神色形态 and 舌诊.

2. 闻诊 (Wén zhěn) - Listening and Smelling: Listening to the patient's voice, breathing, coughing, and smelling odors from the body or secretions.

3. 问诊 (Wèn zhěn) - Inquiry: Asking about symptoms, medical history, lifestyle, etc. The classic "十问" (ten questions) covers things like cold/heat sensations, sweating, appetite, sleep, bowel/bladder function, etc.

4. 切诊 (Qiè zhěn) - Pulse-taking and Palpation: Feeling the pulse (脉诊) and palpating the body (按诊). Pulse diagnosis involves feeling the radial artery at the wrist and distinguishing many pulse qualities (like 浮沉迟数 etc.)

四诊合参 means the physician must combine all four methods' findings for comprehensive analysis, rather than relying on just one method, to make an accurate diagnosis (辨证). This concept emphasizes that each diagnostic method has its limitations and they complement each other. The phrase comes from classical texts; 《难经》 mentioned "望而知之谓之神，闻而知之谓之圣，问而知之谓之工，切脉而知之谓之巧" - showing the four methods' importance.

The rationale: Each of the four methods reflects different aspects of the patient's condition. Only by integrating all information can the doctor form a complete picture for 辨证论治 (syndrome differentiation and treatment). Relying on a single method can lead to misdiagnosis. For example, 《伤寒论》 emphasizes pulse and symptoms together.
