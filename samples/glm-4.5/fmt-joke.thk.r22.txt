The wife/apple/watermelon joke is a classic Chinese programmer joke that translates well. Let me write it out:

程序员的老婆对他说:"下班回来的路上买十个包子,如果看到卖西瓜的,就买一个。"
程序员回到家,手里拿着一个包子。
老婆问:"你怎么只买了一个包子?"
程序员:"因为我看到了卖西瓜的。"

This is a great one - the programmer interpreted "买一个" (buy one) as referring to the conditional, so when he saw watermelon sellers, he bought one watermelon... wait, actually the joke is he bought one baozi instead of ten because he saw the watermelon seller, and the "buy one" applied... hmm, let me think again.

The original: "买十个包子,如果看到卖西瓜的,就买一个" - Buy 10 baozi, if you see watermelon sellers, buy one.
The programmer interprets it like code: if (看到卖西瓜的) { 买一个 } - so "buy one" replaces... no wait.

Actually the classic version: he comes back with one baozi (or ten watermelons in some versions). The logic: the programmer treats "买一个" as modifying the previous command due to his code-brain. Like in code:

买十个包子
if (看到卖西瓜的) 买一个

The ambiguity: does "买一个" mean buy one watermelon, or does it modify the baozi count? The programmer interprets it as the number of baozi to buy being conditional... Actually the most common version of the punchline: he comes back with 1 baozi, and when asked why, says "因为我看到了卖西瓜的" (because I saw the watermelon seller) - meaning he interpreted the instruction as: buy 10 baozi UNLESS you see watermelon sellers, in which case buy 1 (baozi).

Some versions have him bring back one watermelon and ten baozi... The most common Chinese internet version: 程序员买了一个包子回来,老婆问为什么只买一个,他说:因为我看到了卖西瓜的。

That's the classic. It's a genuinely funny cold joke for programmers.
