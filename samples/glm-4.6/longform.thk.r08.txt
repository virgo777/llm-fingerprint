四诊合参 is a fundamental diagnostic principle in Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM). The "four diagnostic methods" (四诊) are:
1. 望诊 (Wàng zhěn) - Inspection/Observation: Observing the patient's spirit (神), complexion (色), body shape (形态), tongue (舌象), etc.
2. 闻诊 (Wén zhěn) - Listening and Smelling: Listening to the patient's voice, breathing, coughing, and smelling odors from the body or secretions.
3. 问诊 (Wèn zhěn) - Inquiry: Asking about symptoms, medical history, lifestyle, pain location, sleep, appetite, etc. The classic "十问" (ten questions).
4. 切诊 (Qiè zhěn) - Pulse-taking and Palpation: Feeling the pulse (脉诊) and palpating the body (按诊).

四诊合参 means that physicians should not rely on just one method but should comprehensively analyze information gathered from all four methods to make an accurate diagnosis (辨证). This is important because:
- Each method has its limitations
- Information from different methods can complement each other
- Sometimes symptoms may conflict, requiring careful synthesis ("舍脉从症" or "舍症从脉" - sometimes prioritizing symptoms over pulse findings or vice versa)

The concept originates from classic texts like 《黄帝内经》 and was emphasized by 张仲景 in 《伤寒杂病论》. The famous phrase "四诊合参" emphasizes holistic analysis.

Historical context: 扁鹊 is often credited as a master of all four diagnostic methods. The saying goes he could 望而知之谓之神, 闻而知之谓之圣, 问而知之谓之工, 切而知之谓之巧.
