四诊合参 refers to the four diagnostic methods in TCM:
1. 望诊 (Wàng zhěn) - Inspection/Observation: observing the patient's spirit (神), complexion (色), body shape (形态), tongue (舌象), etc.
2. 闻诊 (Wén zhěn) - Listening and Smelling: listening to voice, breathing, and smelling odors
3. 问诊 (Wèn zhěn) - Inquiry: asking about symptoms, medical history, lifestyle, etc. (十问歌)
4. 切诊 (Qiè zhěn) - Pulse-taking and Palpation: feeling the pulse (脉诊) and palpating the body (按诊)

The concept of 四诊合参 emphasizes that these four methods should be used together and integrated, not relied upon individually. Each method has its own scope and limitations; only by combining all the information from all four methods can a physician make an accurate diagnosis (辨证).

The origin: The four diagnostic methods were established in the Huangdi Neijing (黄帝内经), and were formalized. 扁鹊 (Bian Que) is traditionally credited. In 《难经》 there's mention of "望而知之谓之神，闻而知之谓之圣，问而知之谓之工，切而知之谓之巧" - this describes the skill levels.

The importance of 合参: 
- Each diagnostic method provides different information
- Some symptoms may conflict (脉证不符), requiring comprehensive analysis
- 舌脉 - tongue and pulse
- The principle "四诊合参" prevents incomplete or biased diagnosis
