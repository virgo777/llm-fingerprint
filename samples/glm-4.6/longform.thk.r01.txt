四诊 refers to the four diagnostic methods in TCM:
1. 望诊 (Wàng zhěn) - Inspection/Observation: observing the patient's spirit (神), complexion (色), body shape (形态), tongue (舌象), etc.
2. 闻诊 (Wén zhěn) - Listening and Smelling: listening to the patient's voice, breathing, coughing, and smelling odors from the body
3. 问诊 (Wèn zhěn) - Inquiry: asking about symptoms, medical history, lifestyle, etc. (十问 - ten questions)
4. 切诊 (Qiè zhěn) - Pulse-taking and Palpation: feeling the pulse (脉诊) and palpating the body (按诊)

合参 means combining/synthesizing all four methods together for comprehensive analysis. The key principle is that a physician should not rely on just one method but should combine all four to make an accurate diagnosis (辨证).

This concept originates from classic texts like 《黄帝内经》 and was emphasized by 《难经》: "望而知之谓之神，闻而知之谓之圣，问而知之谓之工，切而知之谓之巧" (One who knows by inspection is called divine; by listening, sagacious; by inquiry, skilled; by pulse-taking, clever).

Also relevant: 《难经·六十一难》mentions this. And the principle "四诊合参" emphasizes that no single method alone is sufficient - neglecting any method could lead to misdiagnosis.

The theoretical basis: The human body is an organic whole (整体观念), and local pathological changes can reflect the overall condition. The exterior (外) reflects the interior (内), "司外揣内" (inferring the internal from the external), "见微知著" (knowing the significant from the subtle).

Why combine all four? Because each method has limitations. For example, pulse conditions can be affected by various factors; some patients may have "舍脉从症" (abandon pulse signs and follow symptoms) or "舍症从脉" (abandon symptoms and follow pulse signs) situations.

The purpose of 四诊合参 is to gather comprehensive clinical information to support 辨证论治 (syndrome differentiation and treatment).
