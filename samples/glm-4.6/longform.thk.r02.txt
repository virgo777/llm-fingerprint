四诊合参 refers to the comprehensive analysis using the four diagnostic methods of TCM:
1. 望诊 (Wàng zhěn) - Inspection/Observation: observing the patient's spirit (神), color (色), physical appearance (形态), tongue (舌象), etc.
2. 闻诊 (Wén zhěn) - Listening and Smelling: listening to the patient's voice, breathing, coughing, and smelling odors
3. 问诊 (Wèn zhěn) - Inquiry: asking about symptoms, medical history, lifestyle, etc. (十问 - ten questions)
4. 切诊 (Qiè zhěn) - Pulse-taking and Palpation: feeling the pulse (脉诊) and palpating the body

The concept of 四诊合参 emphasizes that these four methods should be used together, not in isolation. Each method provides different information, and only by combining all four can a physician make an accurate diagnosis (辨证 - syndrome differentiation). This reflects the holistic view of TCM.

The origin can be traced back to 《黄帝内经》 and was systematized by 扁鹊, and later 《难经》 stated "望而知之谓之神，闻而知之谓之圣，问而知之谓之工，切脉而知之谓之巧" (knowing by inspection is called divine, by listening is called sagely, by inquiry is called skilled, by pulse-taking is called clever).

明代张景岳 in 《景岳全书》 emphasized "四诊合参" - he compiled the "十问歌" (Ten Questions Song).

The principle is that if one relies on only one method, it may lead to misdiagnosis. 《难经》 says "不以四诊俱备，何以为医" - something like that. Actually the phrase is from 《医宗金鉴》: "四诊心法" states that doctors should combine all four methods.
