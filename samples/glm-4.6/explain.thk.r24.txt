Bioelectrical Impedance Analysis (BIA) is a method used to estimate body composition, including body fat, lean body mass, total body water, and other components. Here's how it works:

1. Principle: It measures the resistance (impedance) of electrical currents as they pass through the body. The basic idea is that different body tissues have different electrical properties:
   - Water and muscle contain electrolytes and conduct electricity well (low impedance)
   - Fat tissue contains less water and conducts electricity poorly (high impedance)

2. Method: Small electrical currents (usually very low and safe) are passed through the body using electrodes. The impedance is measured at different frequencies.

3. Application: It's commonly used in body composition analyzers, smart scales, and medical devices to estimate:
   - Body fat percentage
   - Muscle mass
   - Total body water
   - Basal metabolic rate
   - Protein levels
   - Mineral content

4. Advantages: Non-invasive, quick, relatively inexpensive, no radiation exposure
5. Limitations: Results can be affected by hydration status, food intake, exercise, and body temperature
