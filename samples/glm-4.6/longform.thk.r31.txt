2. 闻诊 (Wén zhěn) - Listening and Smelling: listening to the patient's voice, breathing, and smelling odors
3. 问诊 (Wèn zhěn) - Inquiry: asking about symptoms, medical history, lifestyle, etc.
4. 切诊 (Qiè zhěn) - Pulse-taking and Palpation: feeling the pulse (脉诊) and palpating the body (按诊)

合参 means combining/synthesizing all these methods together for comprehensive analysis.

The principle: Each of the four methods provides different information about the patient's condition. A TCM practitioner must combine all four to make an accurate diagnosis (辨证). Relying on just one method can lead to errors. There's a saying: "望而知之谓之神，闻而知之谓之圣，问而知之谓之工，切而知之谓之巧" from 《难经》.

The concept originates from classical texts like 《黄帝内经》(Huangdi Neijing). The idea is 诸诊合用，四诊并用 - no single diagnostic method should be neglected.
